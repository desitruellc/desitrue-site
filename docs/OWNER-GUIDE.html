01

How your site works

Your website is a handful of plain files: four pages, one stylesheet and some images. They live in a repository (a folder with full history) on GitHub. GitHub's free hosting service, GitHub Pages, puts those files on the internet. GoDaddy holds your domain name and tells browsers where to find the site.

What it costs

GitHub and GitHub Pages: $0. The only bill is the yearly domain renewal at GoDaddy. Claude Code is optional and needs a paid Claude plan (see section 5).

What it can't do

The site has no database and no server of its own, so it can't take payments or store form entries. Booking goes through Calendly, and email and phone links open the visitor's own apps.

03

Everyday changes

Each change below can be made in two ways: ask Claude Code in plain words (section 5), or edit the file yourself on github.com (section 4). In each snippet, change only the words you mean to change. Keep the angle brackets < >, quote marks and equals signs exactly as they are.

Change a sentence

Find the sentence in the right page file and retype it.

Before · index.html
<p class="hero-note">Working with small businesses,
busy professionals and families.</p>
After
<p class="hero-note">Serving clients across
[your region] since 2020.</p>
On the home page, change the line under the buttons to "Serving clients across your area since 2020."

Fill in your email and phone everywhere

The placeholders [email] and [phone] appear in the footer of all four pages (index.html, about.html, contact.html, 404.html) and in the Contact page's details box. Each of those spots has it twice: once inside the link and once as the text people see. The Contact page's "Email to book" button also has [email] inside its link. This change is quickest with Claude; on github.com, edit each of the four files in turn. Phone links need the number written without spaces, with the country code: tel:+15555550123.

Before
<a href="mailto:[email]">[email]</a>
<a href="tel:[phone]">[phone]</a>
After
<a href="mailto:hello@desitrue.com">hello@desitrue.com</a>
<a href="tel:+15555550123">(555) 555-0123</a>
Replace every [email] on the site with hello@desitrue.com and every [phone] with (555) 555-0123. Use +15555550123 inside the tel: links.

Swap in a photo

No code to change. Make the image (prompts are in ASSETS.html), crop and resize it to the size listed there, name it exactly as listed, and put it in assets/img/, replacing any file with the same name. Until a photo exists, the large arched frames show a soft rose-gold shape on wide screens, and everything else (the service card photos, and the arches on phones) is simply hidden. If you use the stand-in About photo from ASSETS.html instead of a real headshot, also ask Claude to change that image's description (its alt text), which currently says it is a portrait of Desirée.

Here is my new headshot (attach the file). Crop it to 4:5, resize it to 1000×1250, save it as assets/img/about-portrait.jpg, and show me the About page.

Turn on Calendly booking

In contact.html, find the line that starts with <div class="calendly-inline-widget" and paste your Calendly link between the two quote marks after data-url=. The calendar appears automatically and the "coming soon" box disappears.

Before · contact.html
<div class="calendly-inline-widget" data-url="" hidden></div>
After
<div class="calendly-inline-widget"
  data-url="https://calendly.com/desitrue/consultation" hidden></div>

Leave the word hidden in place; the page removes it itself once a link is there. Your Calendly link is under Share on any event type in Calendly.

Add a service

Each service card on the home page has a list of ticked items. Copy one whole <li>…</li> line, paste it on a new line of its own lined up with the others, and change the words.

Before · index.html
<li>Cash-flow forecasting</li>
<li>Tax preparation</li>
After
<li>Cash-flow forecasting</li>
<li>Payroll</li>
<li>Tax preparation</li>
Add "Payroll" to the Money & bookkeeping card on the home page.
04

Publishing a change

A change goes live when it is committed (saved into the history with a short note) and pushed (uploaded to GitHub). About a minute later, GitHub Pages republishes the site. Every earlier version is kept, so any mistake can be undone.

Quick fix on github.com, nothing to install

  1. Open the fileGo to your repository on github.com and click the file, for example contact.html.
  2. Click the pencil iconIt's at the top right of the file ("Edit this file").
  3. Make the changeUse Ctrl+F to find the text.
  4. Click "Commit changes…"Write a short note such as "Update phone number", keep "Commit directly to the main branch" selected, and confirm.
  5. Wait a minute and refresh the sitePress Ctrl+F5 to skip your browser's saved copy.

No preview on github.com. Edits made this way go live without a preview, so keep them to small text fixes. For anything else, use Claude Code, which shows you the page before publishing (section 5).

Checking that it published

In the repository, open the Actions tab. The newest run, called pages build and deployment, shows a green tick when the site is live, or a red cross if something went wrong (see section 8).

Making changes with Claude Code on Windows

Claude Code is an AI assistant that works directly in your website folder. You describe the change in plain words ("add a testimonials section that matches the design"); it edits the files, shows you a preview, and publishes the change when you say so. It's the easiest way to make anything bigger than a one-line edit.

What you need. A Windows 10 or 11 PC (64-bit). A paid Claude plan: Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise; the free plan doesn't include Claude Code. And a free GitHub account, which you will have anyway once the site is yours (section 6).

One-time setup (about 15 minutes)

  1. Install the Claude desktop app.Download it from claude.com/download (Windows), run the installer, open Claude from the Start menu and sign in with your Claude account.
  2. Install Git for Windows.Download it from git-scm.com/download/win and click through the installer with the default choices. Git is the tool that saves versions and uploads them to GitHub. You won't need to learn it, because Claude runs it for you. It also handles your GitHub sign-in: the first time something is uploaded, a browser window asks you to log in to GitHub.
  3. Open the Code tab.In the Claude app, click Code at the top. Choose Local, click Select folder, and pick (or create) a folder such as Documents\Websites. If the Code tab asks you to upgrade, your plan doesn't include Claude Code.
  4. Download the site.Ask Claude:
    Clone https://github.com/DesiTrueLLC/desitrue-site into this folder.
    Then start a new session with Select folder pointing at the new desitrue-site folder. From then on, always open that folder.
  5. Pick the safe mode.Choose the Manual (ask before each change) permission mode. Claude then shows each change as a before-and-after and waits for you to click Accept.

Prefer a terminal? The command-line version installs from PowerShell (not as Administrator) with irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex. Open a new window afterwards and type claude. Sources: desktop quickstart, setup and requirements. Checked October 2026.

How a change goes

Things to ask

Change my phone number to (555) 555-0123 on every page.
Add a testimonials section to the home page, above "Meet the founder", that matches the design. Use these three quotes: …
The headline feels too long on a phone. Show me two shorter options.
Show me the contact page in the Browser pane at phone size.
That looks good. Commit it with a short note and push it.
Undo the last change I published.

Good habits

  • Start fresh. Begin each session with "pull the latest changes from GitHub", so you're never working on an old copy.
  • Preview before publishing. End requests with "show me before you push".
  • One change at a time. Small changes are easy to check and easy to undo.
  • Everything here is public. The repository and the website can both be seen by anyone (that's what keeps hosting free), so never ask Claude to put passwords, client details or private documents in this folder.
  • Leave the domain settings alone. Don't ask Claude to change the CNAME file or anything in GoDaddy. Those are set once on domain move day (section 7).

07

Domain move day

Right now desitrue.com points at a Google Site. Once the domain is managed at GoDaddy, one change in GoDaddy's DNS settings points it at the new site instead. Your email is separate: it is routed by different records, which must stay exactly as they are.

Protect your email. Before the domain transfer, take a screenshot of every DNS record at the current provider. If the DNS is hosted where the domain is registered today (Squarespace, formerly Google Domains), the records do not move to GoDaddy with the domain, and GoDaddy starts with its own defaults. Email then stops arriving until every email record (MX, the TXT records, and any email CNAMEs) is re-entered in GoDaddy exactly as it was.

The steps

  1. Prove the domain is yours to GitHub.On github.com: your profile picture → Settings → Pages → Add a domain → enter desitrue.com. GitHub shows a TXT record. Add it in GoDaddy's DNS page (Domain Portfolio → desitrue.com → DNS; the menu names change now and then), wait a few minutes, then click Verify. This stops anyone else's GitHub site from claiming your domain.
  2. Tell GitHub the domain.Repository → Settings → Pages → Custom domain: type desitrue.com and Save. GitHub adds a file called CNAME to the site for you. Go straight on to the next step.
  3. Point the domain at GitHub.In GoDaddy's DNS page, make the records match the table below. GitHub's DNS check then passes on its own, which can take from a few minutes to a few hours.
  4. Turn on the padlock.When the check passes, go back to the repository's Settings → Pages and tick Enforce HTTPS. The certificate can take up to an hour to appear.
  5. Finish with Claude.
    Pull the latest changes from GitHub first. The site is now live at desitrue.com. Update the og:image tags on every page to the full address https://desitrue.com/assets/img/og-image.jpg, then commit and push.

08

Troubleshooting

I published a change but the site looks the same.
Wait two minutes, then press Ctrl+F5. Check the Actions tab for a green tick. A red cross usually means the publish step failed; click it to see why, or ask Claude to look.
GitHub says "Domain's DNS record could not be retrieved" or "improperly configured".
DNS changes can take a few hours to spread. Re-check the table in section 7, especially that no old A or CNAME record for @ or www is left over. Then click Check again in Settings → Pages.
A photo doesn't show.
The file name must match exactly, including lowercase letters and .jpg (not .jpeg or .JPG), and the file must be inside assets/img/.
The Calendly calendar doesn't appear.
Check that the link in contact.html starts with https://calendly.com/ and sits between the quotes after data-url=. Some ad blockers hide Calendly; try a private window.
The "page not found" page looks unstyled.
That's expected on the temporary preview address: the not-found page's styling and links only work at desitrue.com, where they point to the right place. Once the domain is live it works normally.
Email stopped arriving after the domain move.
The MX or email TXT records were changed or removed. Put them back exactly as in your "before" screenshot (section 7).
Something broke and I want the old version back.
Nothing is ever lost. Ask Claude: "Undo my last published change." On github.com you can also open the file, click History, and copy the older version back.
